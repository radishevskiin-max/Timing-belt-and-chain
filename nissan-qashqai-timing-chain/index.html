Why the Qashqai 1.2 / 1.3 DIG-T timing chain needs attention

The small turbo petrol engines in the Qashqai use a timing chain instead of a belt. The chain is meant to last a long time, but in real life it can stretch early – especially with long service intervals, the wrong oil or lots of short journeys.

• Rattle for a few seconds after a cold start.
• Engine warning light, fault codes P0011 / P0016.
• Rough idle, hesitation or loss of power.
• Worse fuel economy as valve timing drifts.
• Rattle at idle when warm, or engine hard to start.

A stretched chain can jump a tooth. On these engines that can mean valves hitting pistons – a repair that costs far more than the chain. Replacing the chain at the first symptoms protects the engine.

Qashqai timing chain – common questions

How do I know my Qashqai timing chain is stretched?

Typical signs are a rattle for a few seconds after a cold start, an engine warning light, camshaft/crankshaft correlation fault codes (P0011, P0016), rough idle or loss of power. Send us your registration and symptoms on WhatsApp and we will advise.

Isn’t a timing chain supposed to last the life of the engine?

In theory, yes. In practice, the chains on these small turbo petrol engines can stretch early, especially with long oil-change intervals and lots of short trips. Replacing a stretched chain early is far cheaper than repairing a damaged engine.

How long does the job take?

How long does a Qashqai 1.2 DIG-T timing chain last?

Nissan does not give a fixed interval, but on the 1.2 DIG-T (HRA2DDT) stretched chains are commonly found from around 60,000–80,000 miles, sometimes earlier on cars with long oil-change intervals.

What does a stretched timing chain sound like?

A metallic rattle or ‘diesel-like’ clatter for a few seconds after a cold start, sometimes also at idle once warm. If the noise is getting longer or louder, have it checked soon.
