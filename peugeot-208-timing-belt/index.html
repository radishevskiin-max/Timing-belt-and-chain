Why timing belt maintenance is critical on Peugeot 208 PureTech

The 1.0 and 1.2 PureTech engines use a timing belt that runs in engine oil. Over time the belt ages, softens and starts to shed tiny rubber particles into the oil.

• Rubber particles can block the oil pick-up strainer in the sump.
• This restricts oil flow and leads to low oil pressure, especially at hot idle.
• Low oil pressure can starve camshafts and crankshaft.
• VVT solenoids may stick, causing rough idle, fault codes and drivability issues.

Ignoring a worn timing belt on these engines doesn’t only risk the belt snapping. It also means:

  • Clogged oil channels and pick-up – possible engine damage if oil pressure drops.
  • Unstable variable valve timing – rough running, fault codes, poor fuel economy.
  • Increased risk of internal engine components wear due to contaminated oil.

That’s why this package is built around the whole oil system, not just the belt itself. Belt, oil, filters, sump, strainer and VVT system are addressed together, so the engine can breathe and lubricate properly again.

Peugeot 208 PureTech timing belt – symptoms of a failing wet belt

If you notice any of these on a 1.0 or 1.2 PureTech engine, the timing belt and oil system should be checked as soon as possible:

  • Oil pressure warning light or “Engine oil pressure too low” Belt debris blocks the oil pick-up strainer – stop driving and get it checked.
  • Hard brake pedal / brake assistance fault Rubber particles can block the vacuum pump feed, so the brake servo loses assistance.
  • “Engine fault – repair needed” or “Anti-pollution fault” Often with fault codes P0011, P0016, P0341 or P0524 (low oil pressure).
  • Rough idle, juddering or loss of power Sticking VVT solenoids caused by contaminated oil.
  • Black bits on the oil dipstick or in the oil filler cap A clear sign the wet belt is breaking up.
  • Belt due by age or mileage Peugeot revised the PureTech wet-belt interval to around 6 years or 50,000 miles – many cars are now overdue.

Peugeot 208 timing belt – common questions

How do I know my Peugeot 208 PureTech timing belt needs replacing?

Typical signs are an oil pressure warning light, a hard brake pedal, “Engine fault – repair needed” or “Anti-pollution fault” messages, rough running and black rubber particles in the oil.

What is the timing belt interval on the 1.2 PureTech?

Peugeot reduced the interval for the PureTech wet belt to roughly every 6 years or 50,000 miles (whichever comes first). If your car has never had the belt changed, it is likely due now.

No. On the PureTech engine this usually means the oil strainer is blocked by belt debris. Driving on can destroy the engine – call or WhatsApp us and we will advise.
